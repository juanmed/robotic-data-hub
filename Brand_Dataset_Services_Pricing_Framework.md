# Brand Dataset Services: Revised Pricing Framework
## Revenue-Based Tiers Benchmarked Against Market Access Costs

**Date:** April 2026  
**Purpose:** Establish data-driven pricing tiers for Brand Dataset Services based on company size, revenue, and comparable market-access investments

---

## Executive Summary

Brand Dataset Services pricing should scale with company size and be positioned as **cost-efficient insurance** compared to existing market-access expenditures. Our research shows brands already spend:

- **Enterprise SEO:** $120K-$1.2M+/year
- **AI Optimization (GEO):** $180K-$600K/year
- **Trade Shows:** $100K-$300K+ per major event
- **Retail Slotting:** $25K-$1M+ per product rollout
- **Digital Advertising:** $600K-$2M+/year (enterprise)
- **PR/Content Marketing:** $120K-$600K+/year
- **Market Research:** $100K-$500K+/year

Against this backdrop, **$50K-$2M/year for comprehensive dataset representation** is a rounding error in most brands' marketing budgets while addressing an emerging existential risk.

---

## Pricing Philosophy

### Three Core Principles

1. **Value-Based, Not Cost-Plus**
   - Price based on customer's avoided costs (custom integration: $50K-$200K per robot platform)
   - Price based on competitive necessity (market share protection)
   - Price based on revenue at risk (sales lost to better-represented competitors)

2. **Segmented by Company Revenue**
   - Tier 1 ($10B+ revenue): Can afford premium pricing, highest risk exposure
   - Tier 2 ($1B-$10B revenue): Mid-market pricing, regional/category leaders
   - Tier 3 ($100M-$1B revenue): Accessible pricing, specialists and emerging brands
   - Tier 4 ($10M-$100M revenue): Entry-level pricing, regional players

3. **Anchored to Marketing Budgets, Not IT Budgets**
   - Compare to SEO, PR, trade shows (strategic visibility)
   - NOT compared to SaaS tools or operational software (tactical efficiency)
   - Positioned as "future market access insurance"

## Revised Pricing Tiers

### TIER 1: GLOBAL ENTERPRISE
**Target Companies:** $10B+ annual revenue (e.g., Samsung, Bosch, Whirlpool, LG)

| Service Package | Annual Fee | Scope |
|---|---:|---|
| **Essential Coverage** | $500K - $800K | Single product category, 5-8 embodiments, 20-30 interaction types, quarterly reports |
| **Category Leadership** | $900K - $1.5M | Full product category, 10-15 embodiments, 40-60 interaction types, monthly reports, competitive intelligence |
| **Portfolio Protection** | $1.6M - $2.5M | Multi-category coverage, 20+ embodiments, 80+ interaction types, weekly monitoring, strategic partnership, co-marketing |

**Justification:**
- These companies spend **$77M-$97M annually on marketing** (7.7% of $1B revenue)
- **Enterprise SEO alone:** $240K-$1.2M/year
- **GEO services:** $300K-$600K/year
- **Single major trade show:** $100K-$300K per event (attend 5-10/year)
- **Amazon advertising:** $180K-$720K+/year
- **Dataset services at $500K-$2.5M = 0.5-2.5% of total marketing budget**

**Pricing Variables:**
- **Revenue multiplier:** $10B-$50B → $500K-$1M base; $50B-$100B → $1M-$1.5M base; $100B+ → $1.5M-$2.5M+
- **Product line breadth:** +$100K-$300K per additional major category
- **Geographic complexity:** +$50K-$150K for multi-region coordination
- **Competitive urgency:** +20-30% premium if category leader already signed

**Variable Add-Ons:**
- Rush dataset commissioning: $50K-$150K per project
- Legacy product retroactive coverage: $30K-$100K per product line
- Specialized embodiment testing: $40K-$100K per embodiment
- Real-time competitive monitoring: +$100K-$200K/year
- White-label brand subsidiary coverage: +$150K-$300K per brand

## Competitive Positioning

### Why This Pricing Is Actually Conservative

**Comparison to Alternative Costs:**

| Alternative Approach | Annual Cost | Coverage Breadth | Risk |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Custom Integration** (per robot platform) | $50K-$200K each | Single platform only | Ongoing per-platform costs |
| **In-House Dataset Team** | $300K-$800K | Limited embodiments | Talent/expertise risk |
| **Reactive "Fix-It-Later"** | $0 upfront | None | Market exclusion risk, lost sales |
| **GamiphyAI Brand Services** | $50K-$2.5M | Multi-platform, comprehensive | Minimal risk |

**ROI Calculation for Tier 1 Customer:**

```
Scenario: $20B revenue appliance manufacturer, $1.2M annual dataset fee

Alternative Costs Avoided:
- Custom integration (10 major robot platforms): $500K-$2M
- In-house dataset team: $500K-$1M annually
- Lost sales from poor robot compatibility: $10M-$50M+ (0.05-0.25% revenue)

Total Avoided Costs: $11M-$53M
Investment: $1.2M
ROI: 9x to 44x
```

**Breakeven Analysis:**

For dataset services to NOT be worth it, a brand would need to believe:
1. **Robots won't handle their products** (contradicts all industry trends)
2. **Their products will be represented anyway** (by whom? their competitors?)
3. **Custom integration is cheaper** (demonstrably false at scale)
4. **Dataset representation won't matter** (ignores how AI/ML systems work)

Given these are all false, **any brand with robot-interaction products should find dataset services a no-brainer investment**.

## Payment Terms & Contract Structure

### Standard Terms

- **Annual subscription** (monthly billing available for Tier 3-4)
- **Multi-year discounts:** 10% off year 2, 15% off year 3+
- **Early payment discount:** 5% for annual prepayment
- **Volume discounts:** 15-20% for multi-brand portfolios (parent companies)

### Contract Flexibility

- **Pilot programs:** 6-month pilots at 50-60% of annual fee (Tier 1-2 only)
- **Ramp pricing:** Year 1 at 70%, Year 2 at 85%, Year 3+ at 100% (growth-stage companies)
- **Performance guarantees:** Success rate thresholds tied to pricing
- **Category exclusivity:** Premium pricing for category exclusivity agreements

### Payment Milestones

- **25% upon contract signature** (strategy and planning)
- **25% upon dataset commissioning start** (Q1)
- **25% upon mid-year review** (Q2)
- **25% upon annual delivery** (Q4)

## Sales Strategy by Tier

### Tier 1: Strategic Partnerships
- **Sales cycle:** 9-18 months
- **Decision makers:** CMO, VP Product, Chief Innovation Officer
- **Proof points:** Competitive case studies, market analysis, executive education
- **Differentiation:** White-glove service, strategic advisory, co-innovation

### Tier 2: Value Selling
- **Sales cycle:** 6-12 months
- **Decision makers:** VP Marketing, Head of Product, Digital Transformation lead
- **Proof points:** ROI calculators, competitor activity, pilot results
- **Differentiation:** Proven methodology, category expertise, scalability

### Tier 3: Solution Selling
- **Sales cycle:** 3-6 months
- **Decision makers:** Marketing Director, Product Manager, Operations VP
- **Proof points:** Quick wins, competitive pressure, accessible pricing
- **Differentiation:** Fast deployment, clear deliverables, measurable results

### Tier 4: Transactional
- **Sales cycle:** 1-3 months
- **Decision makers:** Founder, Marketing Manager, Business Development
- **Proof points:** Entry pricing, platform access, growth enablement
- **Differentiation:** Low barrier to entry, self-service options, community

## Conclusion: Pricing That Reflects Value

The revised pricing framework:

1. **Scales appropriately** with company size and marketing budgets
2. **Anchors to comparable costs** brands already accept as strategic investments
3. **Reflects competitive dynamics** through premium pricing for urgent scenarios
4. **Enables market entry** at all tiers while maximizing revenue from enterprise customers
5. **Justifies investment** through clear ROI vs. alternatives

**Key Message for Sales:**
> "You already spend $X million annually ensuring customers can discover your products through Google, trade shows, and retail. As robots become the discovery and interaction layer for physical products, dataset representation is the same strategic imperative—but **at a fraction of your existing market-access costs**."

This positioning transforms dataset services from "new technology spend" to "essential infrastructure" comparable to SEO, which is now a non-negotiable budget line item for every consumer-facing brand.
